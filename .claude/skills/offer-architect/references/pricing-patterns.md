# Pricing patterns

## 1. Anchor pricing (the most-used)

Show a higher tier first. The next tier feels reasonable by comparison.

| Tier | Price | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Premium / Done-for-you | $9,997 | Anchor — most won't buy this |
| Core | $1,997 | What 80% buy |
| Tripwire | $97 | Front-end / list builder |

Without the $9,997 anchor, $1,997 feels expensive. With it, $1,997 feels like the smart middle.

## 2. Charm pricing

Prices ending in 7 or 9 outperform round numbers in B2C and info products. $97, $497, $1,997 land harder than $100, $500, $2,000. In B2B and high-ticket, round numbers signal premium ($10,000 reads better than $9,997 to an enterprise buyer).

## 3. Decoy pricing

Add a deliberately worse option to make the target tier look obvious.

> "Subscribe digital only for $59. Subscribe digital + print for $125. Subscribe print only for $125."

Nobody buys "print only" — but it makes "digital + print" feel like a steal. Use this on order forms.

## 4. Payment plan splitting

For high-ticket: "$2,997 today" vs "3 payments of $1,000". The split feels easier even though it's more expensive. Always display BOTH and let the buyer pick.

## 5. Founder / charter pricing

For new offers: "Charter pricing — first 50 only. Locks in for life." Drives urgency and seeds the case-study cohort.

## 6. Tripwire mechanics

A $7–$47 tripwire converts cold traffic to buyers. Goal isn't profit on the tripwire — it's:
- Buyer indoctrination (now they're a customer, not a lead)
- Stripe relationship (their card is on file)
- Higher AOV via order bump + upsell (often 30–60% of buyers add one)

## 7. Three-option pricing rules

- 3 tiers > 4 tiers > 2 tiers > 1 tier (psychology of "the middle option")
- Highlight the middle tier ("most popular" badge)
- The premium tier should be 2–4× the middle tier's price

## 8. Value stack mismatch

The stack's total stated value should be 3–5× the offer price. Less than 3× and the offer feels weak. More than 10× and people don't believe the math.

## 9. Bonus stacking timing

Stack bonuses AFTER the main price reveal. "And you also get…" lifts conversion. Stacking before dilutes the price anchor.

## 10. Fast-action vs evergreen

- Fast-action bonuses ("decide in next 15 minutes, get [extra]") work on live launches
- Evergreen offers should have **structural** scarcity (limited cohort size, license-only, monthly ceiling) — not fake countdown timers
