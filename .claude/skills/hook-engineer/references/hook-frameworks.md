# Hook frameworks

The 7 hook patterns that survive cold traffic. Mix them by awareness level.

## 1. AIDA — Attention, Interest, Desire, Action

Classic. Best at awareness levels 2–3.

> "[Specific stat] is wrong about [topic]. Here's what [audience] actually need to know — and the [outcome] that comes from it."

## 2. PAS — Problem, Agitation, Solution

Best at awareness level 4 (problem-aware). Names the pain, twists the knife, then offers the way out.

> "[Problem in their language]. [What it's costing them]. Here's the [unique mechanism] that fixes it."

## 3. BAB — Before, After, Bridge

Best for transformation offers (fitness, business coaching, skill acquisition).

> "[Where they are now]. [Where they could be]. The [bridge] is [unique mechanism]."

## 4. Curiosity gap

Best at awareness level 5 (unaware) — earn the click on cold ads.

> "Why [specific result] happens to [specific group] — and [counterintuitive truth]."
> "I [did unexpected thing] and [unexpected result] — here's what I learned."

## 5. Story / pattern interrupt

For the very top of the funnel. Wraps the hook in a narrative beat that breaks scroll.

> "[Time marker] [unexpected character] [unexpected action]. [Outcome that surprised everyone]."
> "Last Tuesday a [character] [action]. The result [outcome]."

## 6. Contrarian / hot take

Polarizing. Lands hardest with audiences who've heard the conventional advice and are skeptical.

> "Most [category] advice is wrong. Here's why."
> "Stop [popular thing]. Start [counter thing] instead."

Use sparingly — once per quarter at most. Burnout if overused.

## 7. Statistic-led / specificity

Numbers earn trust faster than adjectives.

> "[N] [audience] in [time period]: [specific result]."
> "Of [N] [things], only [small N] [outcome] — here's why."

Specific numbers beat round numbers ("847" > "800", "$1,997" > "$2,000").

---

## Hook quality rules

- **No questions where the answer is obvious yes/no.** "Are you struggling with X?" → cut.
- **First 3 words carry 80% of the click.** Front-load the most charged word.
- **Specificity > intensity.** "Mary, 47, in Cleveland" beats "many people".
- **One idea per hook.** A hook that promises three things delivers none.
- **Voice match.** Read each hook in the client's voice. If it doesn't sound like them, rewrite.

## Lading-page sequence (where each hook goes)

- **Above the fold (Level 3–4 prospects on cold ad traffic):** PAS or curiosity gap
- **Mid-page (objection handling):** Contrarian or BAB
- **Pre-CTA (decision moment):** Statistic-led or AIDA's Desire→Action close
- **Order form (Level 1):** offer + scarcity, no hook needed
